5.1.1 · Work

Work = force × displacement in the direction of the force

W = F s cos θ   (θ = angle between F and s; unit J = N m)

Work done is the energy transferred by a force. 1 J is the work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application 1 m in the direction of the force.

Worked example

A sledge is pulled 50 m along flat ground by a rope at 30° above the horizontal with tension 80 N. Only the horizontal component does work: W = 80 cos 30° × 50 = 3.46 × 10³ J.

When is no work done?

  • Force perpendicular to motion (cos 90° = 0): e.g. the normal force on a car on a flat road, or the centripetal force in circular motion.
  • No displacement: holding a heavy bag still does no work on the bag.
Explain it to a 5-year-old

In physics, "work" only counts if your push actually moves something along the way you're pushing. Pushing a wall all day? Zero work, even though you're tired!

5.1.2 · Conservation

Energy is never created or destroyed

Principle of conservation of energyEnergy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one form to another. The total energy of a closed system is constant.
SituationEnergy transfer
Ball falling (no air resistance)GPE → KE
Ball falling at terminal velocityGPE → thermal energy (work done against drag); KE constant
Car brakingKE → thermal energy in brakes
Inelastic collisionSome KE → thermal, sound, deformation
Rifle firingChemical → KE of bullet and rifle (+ thermal, sound)

The key working equation in problems: energy at start = energy at end + work done against resistive forces.

Explain it to a 5-year-old

Energy is like sweets passed around a circle of friends. Sweets move from hand to hand, but the total number of sweets never changes. Some might fall on the floor (heat), but they still exist.

5.2.1 and 5.2.2 · Gravitational potential energy

Deriving ΔEP = mgΔh from W = Fs

  1. Lift a mass m at constant speed through height Δh. The upward force needed equals the weight: F = mg.
  2. The force and the displacement are in the same direction, so work done W = FΔh = mgΔh.
  3. This work is stored as gravitational potential energy: ΔEP = mgΔh.

Valid in a uniform gravitational field (g constant), which is true for heights small compared with Earth's radius.

Worked example

A 60 kg student climbs stairs of vertical height 3.0 m. ΔEP = 60 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 1770 J. The length of the staircase does not matter, only the vertical height.

Trap

On a slope, Δh is the vertical rise, not the length along the slope. For a 5.0 m slope at 30°, Δh = 5.0 sin 30° = 2.5 m.

Explain it to a 5-year-old

When you carry a ball up the stairs, you "charge it up" with energy. When it rolls back down, that stored energy comes out as movement.

5.2.3 and 5.2.4 · Kinetic energy

Deriving EK = ½mv² from the equations of motion

  1. A constant resultant force F acts on mass m, initially at rest, over a distance s. Work done W = Fs.
  2. Newton's second law: F = ma.
  3. Equation of motion with u = 0: v² = 2as, so s = v² / 2a.
  4. W = Fs = ma × v²/2a = ½mv². This work becomes kinetic energy: EK = ½mv².

Worked example

A 1200 kg car speeds up from 10 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹. ΔEK = ½ × 1200 × (20² − 10²) = ½ × 1200 × 300 = 1.8 × 10⁵ J.

Trap: (Δv)² instead of Δ(v²)

½ × 1200 × (20 − 10)² = 6.0 × 10⁴ J is wrong. Find each KE, then subtract.

v is squared

Double the speed and KE becomes 4 times bigger. That is why braking distance rises so quickly with speed.

5.1.2 applied · Solving with energy

Energy methods skip the time and the direction

Worked example 1: slope with friction

A 3.0 kg block slides from rest down a slope 5.0 m long with a vertical drop of 2.0 m. Friction is a constant 4.0 N. Find its speed at the bottom.

GPE lost = 3.0 × 9.81 × 2.0 = 58.9 J work against friction = 4.0 × 5.0 = 20.0 J (distance along the slope) KE gained = 58.9 − 20.0 = 38.9 J ½ × 3.0 × v² = 38.9 → v = 5.09 m s⁻¹

Worked example 2: no friction

A pendulum bob is released from 0.45 m above its lowest point. mgh = ½mv², so m cancels: v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.45) = 2.97 m s⁻¹, whatever the mass.

Explain it to a 5-year-old

On a slide, you start with "height energy". At the bottom, it has turned into "speed energy". If the slide is sticky, some of it turns into warm bottom instead, so you go slower.

5.1.3 and 5.1.4 · Efficiency

How much of the input is useful

EfficiencyThe ratio of useful energy output from a system to the total energy input. It can also be written with power.
efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input (× 100%)

Worked example

An electric motor lifts a 50 kg load through 12 m in 20 s. Its electrical input power is 400 W.

useful output = mgΔh = 50 × 9.81 × 12 = 5886 J total input = P × t = 400 × 20 = 8000 J efficiency = 5886 / 8000 = 0.736 = 73.6% useful power = 5886 / 20 = 294 W

The other 2114 J is wasted, mostly as thermal energy in the motor.

Trap

Efficiency above 100% means you have swapped input and output. Efficiency has no unit.

Explain it to a 5-year-old

You pour 10 cups of juice into a funnel, but 3 spill on the floor. 7 out of 10 reached the bottle. That's 70% efficient.

5.1.5 and 5.1.6 · Power

Power is the rate of doing work

PowerWork done per unit time (rate of energy transfer). Unit W = J s⁻¹.
P = W / t

Worked example

The 60 kg student runs up the 3.0 m stairs in 4.0 s. P = mgΔh / t = 1770 / 4.0 = 441 W. Walking up in 12 s, the same work gives only 147 W.

Unit trickMeaning
1 W1 J transferred every second
kW, MW10³ W, 10⁶ W
Base units of Wkg m² s⁻³
Explain it to a 5-year-old

Two kids carry the same box up the stairs. One runs, one walks. They did the same work, but the runner was more powerful because he did it faster.

5.1.7 · Derivation and use

Deriving P = Fv

  1. P = W / t (definition of power).
  2. W = Fs when force and displacement are in the same direction.
  3. P = Fs / t, and s / t = v, so P = Fv.

Here F is the force doing the work (e.g. the driving force of the engine), and v is the velocity in the direction of that force at that instant.

Worked example 1: constant speed on the level

A car travels at a constant 25 m s⁻¹ against a total resistive force of 800 N. Constant velocity means driving force = resistance = 800 N. P = 800 × 25 = 2.0 × 10⁴ W.

Worked example 2: up a hill

A 1000 kg car climbs a 5.0° slope at a constant 20 m s⁻¹. Resistive force 500 N.

component of weight down slope = 1000 × 9.81 × sin 5.0° = 855 N driving force = 500 + 855 = 1355 N P = 1355 × 20 = 2.71 × 10⁴ W

5.1.7 · Power and acceleration

Why a car has a top speed

At maximum power, driving force F = P / v. As v rises, F falls, while drag rises. Top speed is reached when driving force = resistive force, so the resultant force and acceleration are zero.

Worked example

A 1200 kg car has a maximum power of 60 kW. (a) Its top speed is 40 m s⁻¹. Find the resistive force at top speed. (b) At 20 m s⁻¹, at maximum power, the resistive force is 900 N. Find the acceleration.

(a) F = P / v = 60 000 / 40 = 1500 N = resistive force (b) driving force = 60 000 / 20 = 3000 N resultant = 3000 − 900 = 2100 N a = 2100 / 1200 = 1.75 m s⁻²

Trap: which F?

In P = Fv use the driving force, not the resultant force. In F = ma use the resultant force. Mixing these up is the most common error in this type of question.

Explain it to a 5-year-old

When you run, the air pushes back harder the faster you go. Eventually you're pushing as hard as you can and the air pushes back just as hard, so you can't go any faster.

Exam traps · Topic 5

Where marks are lost

Patterns from 9702 examiner reports and mark schemes for this topic. The Past papers slide shows the exact questions.

Work done definition

"Force × distance" without direction.

Force × distance moved in the direction of the force.

Perpendicular forces

Calculating work for a force at 90° to the motion.

Perpendicular force does zero work.

Slope height

Using slope length in mgh.

Vertical height for GPE; slope length for work against friction.

Δ(v²) vs (Δv)²

½m(v − u)² for change in KE.

½mv² − ½mu².

Power definition

"Energy per second" or "work × time".

Work done per unit time.

P = Fv with resultant force

Using F − resistance in P = Fv.

P = (driving force) × v.

Efficiency the wrong way up

Answer above 100%, or a unit given.

useful out ÷ total in, no unit.

"Energy is lost"

In explanations, "lost" without saying where it goes.

"Transferred to thermal energy (work done against friction/air resistance)".
